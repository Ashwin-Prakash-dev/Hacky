# Startathon Copy Persona

## Who we're writing for

A student in Kerala who is already building something — or desperately wants to. They're tired of college fests that hand out certificates. They follow founders on Twitter. They've watched YC videos at 2am. They know what Anthropic and Palantir are. They just haven't had a room to prove themselves in yet.

---

## Voice

**YC-blunt meets buildspace-authentic.**

Write like a founder who's been in the room is texting a friend about it. Not a marketing team. Not an event committee. One person who knows what this is, talking directly to someone who should be there.

- Short sentences. One idea per sentence.
- No adjectives that aren't earned. Not "amazing" or "incredible" or "unique opportunity."
- Specificity over hype. "20 teams" beats "a select group of talented individuals."
- End on conviction. Don't trail off. Say the thing.

---

## Tone rules

**Do this:**
- Direct. "You have to be in the room. That's the whole point."
- Confident without being arrogant. "Kerala has the talent. It's always had it."
- Earned scarcity. "Not everyone gets in." — only works because it's true.
- Personal invite. "If that's you, you should be here."
- Blunt outcomes. "Get hired on the spot." Not "explore potential career opportunities."

**Never do this:**
- "Kerala's most curated hackathon experience" — sounds like a brochure
- "Meaningful engagement" — meaningless
- "Aspiring entrepreneurs" — no one calls themselves that
- Em dashes — use a period or rewrite the sentence
- Exclamation marks — ever
- "We are excited to announce" — delete on sight

---

## The core narrative

Builders change the world. Every company that matters started with someone who just wouldn't stop building — in a dorm, in a garage, in a small room somewhere. Kerala has those people. Startathon is the room we're building for them.

This isn't a hackathon where you show up, get a t-shirt, and list it on your resume. It's 30 hours where the people who can actually change your career are watching you work. If you're serious about building, there is no reason not to apply.

---

## Sentence patterns that work

| Instead of | Write |
|---|---|
| "An opportunity to showcase your skills" | "Show what you can build." |
| "Network with industry professionals" | "Talk to people who've actually done it." |
| "Curated teams selected for excellence" | "20 teams. We pick who's serious." |
| "Explore your potential" | "Find out what you're capable of." |
| "We welcome all students" | "If you want to build something real, apply." |
| "Prizes and recognition await" | "₹2L+ in prizes. First place takes ₹1L+." |

## Section-by-section intent

**Hero** — One punch. Create tension immediately. "Not everyone gets in" does more than a paragraph of features.

**VideoCards** — Let the companies speak. The captions are proof points, not descriptions. Show the pattern: ordinary people, extraordinary output.

**What is Startathon** — Answer it plainly. No spin. Just what happens and why it matters.

**StudentHook** — This is the pitch to the student. Make it feel like a founder is pulling them aside and telling them something real. End with a direct invite.

**Outcome cards** — Concrete. Specific. What actually happens to you if you show up and build well.

**Timeline cards** — Functional. Tell them what they need to know. Flip reveals reward curiosity.

**FAQ** — Answer the question that was actually asked. Shortest possible answer that's still complete.

**Sponsors** — Different reader, same voice. Swap "you" from student to company. Sell the room, not the logo placement.

---

## The one-line filter

Before publishing any copy, ask: **would a founder say this out loud to a friend?**

If the answer is no, rewrite it.
